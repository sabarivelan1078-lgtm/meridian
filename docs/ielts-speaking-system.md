# IELTS Speaking Training System

## IELTS focus
The app targets IELTS General Training Speaking. The IELTS Speaking test is common to IELTS Academic and IELTS General Training, so the app should model the official three-part Speaking structure.

## Speaking test structure

### Part 1: Introduction and interview
Purpose: short personal answers about familiar topics.

Training goals:
- Answer naturally without memorized scripts.
- Extend answers with reasons and examples.
- Reduce fillers and long hesitation.
- Build confidence on common personal topics.

Example topics:
- Work and study.
- Hometown.
- Family.
- Food.
- Transport.
- Technology.
- Hobbies.
- Daily routine.

### Part 2: Long turn cue card
Purpose: speak for up to two minutes after one minute of preparation.

Training goals:
- Plan quickly.
- Tell a coherent story.
- Use past, present, and future tenses accurately.
- Keep speaking without stopping too early.
- Add sensory details and personal reflection.

### Part 3: Discussion
Purpose: answer more abstract, analytical, and opinion-based questions.

Training goals:
- Develop opinions with reasons.
- Compare different viewpoints.
- Speculate about future trends.
- Use complex grammar naturally.
- Give relevant examples without over-explaining.

## Initial diagnostic
The diagnostic should gather enough speech to estimate the user's level.

Recommended diagnostic tasks:
1. Five Part 1 questions.
2. One Part 2 cue card.
3. Three Part 3 questions.
4. One pronunciation reading sample.
5. One free speaking prompt.

Diagnostic outputs:
- Overall estimated speaking band.
- Fluency and coherence estimate.
- Lexical resource estimate.
- Grammatical range and accuracy estimate.
- Pronunciation estimate.
- Confidence level for the estimate.
- Top three strengths.
- Top three weaknesses.
- First seven-day training focus.

## Daily 15-minute lesson
The default daily lesson is intentionally short because the learner selected 15 minutes per day.

Recommended structure:
1. Warm-up prompt: 2 minutes.
2. Micro skill lesson: 3 minutes.
3. IELTS speaking task: 6 minutes.
4. AI feedback review: 3 minutes.
5. Reflection and next action: 1 minute.

## Weekly cycle
A balanced weekly cycle should avoid repetition while maintaining enough practice.

Example week:
- Day 1: Part 1 fluency.
- Day 2: Pronunciation clarity.
- Day 3: Vocabulary and collocations.
- Day 4: Grammar repair in speech.
- Day 5: Part 2 cue card.
- Day 6: Part 3 discussion.
- Day 7: Mini mock and weekly review.

## 100-day plan phases

### Days 1-10: Baseline and confidence
- Complete diagnostic.
- Learn test format.
- Build recording habit.
- Fix urgent speaking blockers.

### Days 11-25: Part 1 fluency
- Give fuller short answers.
- Use reasons and examples.
- Reduce translation pauses.
- Build high-frequency topic vocabulary.

### Days 26-40: Part 2 cue card control
- Plan in one minute.
- Speak for 90-120 seconds.
- Improve story structure.
- Strengthen past tense accuracy.

### Days 41-55: Grammar and vocabulary upgrade
- Build complex sentences.
- Practice collocations and paraphrasing.
- Repair Tamil-to-English grammar transfer issues.
- Use vocabulary naturally in recorded answers.

### Days 56-70: Part 3 discussion depth
- Explain abstract ideas.
- Compare, speculate, and justify opinions.
- Improve coherence under pressure.

### Days 71-85: Mock-test training
- Run timed mini mocks.
- Simulate examiner follow-ups.
- Stabilize score estimates.

### Days 86-100: Band 8 readiness
- Repair remaining weaknesses.
- Practice full mocks.
- Build test-day confidence.
- Review final personalized checklist.

## Missed-day adaptation
The plan should adapt without guilt.

Rules:
- One missed day: offer resume, quick catch-up, or rebalance.
- Two to three missed days: compress low-priority tasks and preserve core speaking tasks.
- More than three missed days: run a mini reassessment and rebuild the next seven days.
- Never stack too many tasks into one day.
- Preserve mock tests and recurring weakness repair.
